## Health notes from uploads and chat, and food advice that respects them

- [x] When any record is uploaded, or the user says or types a note, the AI replies with what it will keep track of from it.
- [x] When the user mentions a health reading or condition, the AI notes it down. For example, "My BP is high now" is saved as a high BP reading with the date.
- [x] When the user mentions an allergy, the AI notes it. For example, "I have an allergy to XYZ" is saved as an allergy to XYZ.
- [x] Keep one list per patient of allergies and medical problems, such as BP, sugar and diabetes.
- [x] When the user asks for food recommendations, the AI uses that list. It never suggests food the patient is allergic to, and it suits the advice to their conditions, for example low salt for high BP and low sugar for diabetes.

## For the parent

- [x] **One big "I'm fine" button each morning.** One tap tells the family all is well. If it is not pressed by 11, the app asks the parent first, and only then tells the family.
- [x] **Talking, not typing.** Everything works by voice, in the parent's own language: "Did I take my evening tablet?", "When is my doctor visit?", "Call my son." Answers are read aloud, slowly, in large text.
- [x] **A plan for today only.** Today's tablets, visits and calls. No dashboards.
- [x] **Help with doctor visits.** Before a visit, a short list of what to tell the doctor, such as "BP was high on 3 days" or "knee pain since Monday". After it, the app reads the prescription back and updates the medicines itself.
- [x] **Refills without thinking.** "You have 5 Vitamin D tablets left. Shall I ask Ravi to order them?" One yes and it is done.
- [x] **Things children can't do from far away.** Pay a bill, order groceries, book a cab to the clinic, find a nurse or physiotherapist nearby. The child can approve and pay from their phone.
- [x] **Company, not only care.** Photos and voice notes from grandchildren in the same chat, reminders of friends' birthdays, a gentle nudge to walk or call someone.
- [x] **A safety net.** Fall detection on the phone or watch, a large SOS button, and a medical card on the lock screen (blood group, allergies, medicines, emergency contact).
- [x] **Dignity and control.** The parent chooses what the family sees. Mood and private chats stay private unless shared.
- [x] **Food advice that knows them.** Allergies, sugar and BP, and the food the family actually cooks. See the health notes section above.

## For the family

- [x] **A weekly summary.** Medicines taken or missed, BP and sugar trends, mood, sleep, how often they chatted, and anything unusual, on one screen in plain words.
- [x] **Early warning, not alarms.** Alert on a change in pattern, such as "missed 3 evening doses this week", "BP rising for 5 days" or "hasn't opened the app since yesterday", not on every small thing.
- [x] **Health readings over time.** BP, sugar, weight and pulse, entered by voice or a photo of the meter, shown as simple graphs to take to the doctor.
- [x] **One medical file.** Prescriptions, lab reports, scans, discharge summaries and insurance in one place, read by AI and searchable ("When was the last HbA1c?"), shareable with any doctor through one link.
- [x] **Sharing care between siblings.** Who visits this month, who pays for what, who went to the last appointment. A shared money view, with expenses split between siblings.
- [x] **A tap-to-call circle.** Doctor, pharmacy, a neighbour, a driver, the building watchman, each one tap away.
- [x] **Emergency mode.** One screen with the nearest hospital, insurance details, medicine list, allergies, blood group and the doctor's number, ready for the ambulance or ER.
- [x] **Video check-ins that just work.** One tap from the child, with auto-answer on the parent's side if the parent has agreed to it.
- [x] **Help with paperwork.** Pension life certificate dates, tax, bank KYC, property tax and insurance renewals.
- [x] **Caring for the carer.** A simple login for a live-in helper or nurse to tick off meals and medicines and flag concerns.
